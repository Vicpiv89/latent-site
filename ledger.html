The ledger

Every flag,
with the date on it

One rule, applied to every season in our database, and a public row for every player it picked out — what we said, when, and what the transfer records say happened next. Rows are never removed and an outcome is never guessed. Where a window is still running the row says so, and counts for nothing.

01 — The rule

Fixed first, applied to everything

A ledger only means something if the rule was written before the outcomes were read, and then run over every season without exception — including the seasons that make it look worse. This is that rule, in full.

Top 20

by Export Index inside his own league and season. Never across competitions.

Age 23

or younger at the last match of that season. The brand is latent value, not proven value.

900 min

minimum in the season, so a flag rests on a real sample rather than a hot month.

2 windows

the outcome is any move to a club outside his league in the two transfer seasons after the flagged one.

What counts as an outcome

Exported — a completed move to a club outside the flagged league, recorded in the transfer data, inside the window. That is all the flag claims. It does not claim the player will succeed after the move: we publish what happened to those minutes below, and on our numbers the flag does not predict them.

What would make this ledger dishonest

Deleting a flag that aged badly, moving the bar after seeing the results, counting a move that happened before the flag date, or filling an unknown outcome with a guess. Each is blocked mechanically: every flag is written to ledger_state.json with the date it first appeared, the rule constants are pinned as ledger-v1, the outcome window starts the season after the flag, and a player the transfer records cannot identify is reported as not observable rather than as a failure.

Both numbers are computed by the same code over the same windows, and a player the transfer records cannot identify is dropped from both sides (11 flags were dropped that way). The sample is small and we are not claiming more than it holds: this is a difference in export rate, on 42 graded flags, published so it can be checked and so it can be watched as it grows.

Disclosed fees on the graded exports, largest first: €4m, €2m, €1.50m, €1.50m and 3 more. 6 of 13 moves were loans, frees or undisclosed — no fee is imputed for those.

The number that does not move — published anyway

Of the graded flags that did export and whose destination minutes are recorded, 3 of 9 went on to hold a regular place (33%, at least 1,350 league minutes). For the comparison group the figure is 25 of 80 (31%). There is no meaningful gap. The flag identifies who moves, not who sticks after moving — which is exactly what the Latent Level's published backtest found, and why no Latent report will ever tell you a player is going to succeed abroad. 04 — How to read it against us

The ways this ledger could still flatter us

Stated by us, before anyone else has to find them.

Identity
Players are matched to transfer records by name and birth year, because our match database holds no transfer-market identifier. A player whose name transliterates differently in the two sources cannot be matched, and reads as not observable — 11 of the flags do. That cuts both ways and is applied identically to the comparison group.
Sample
42 graded flags is a small number. The gap between the flagged and comparison rates is not established at this sample size; it is published so it can be watched, not so it can be quoted as a hit rate.
Retrospective
Flags from seasons that ended before this page first went up were produced by running the rule over history, not published in advance. They are marked by their season, and the rule was fixed before the outcomes were read — but only flags first published from 2026-08-15 onward are a forward record, and that is the part that will matter in a year.
Coverage
The transfer data covers moves in and out of our own competitions. A flagged player who moved between two clubs neither of which touches our leagues would not appear, though by construction that is rare for a player flagged while playing in one of them.
Outcome choice
"Exported" is the outcome because it is the one our data can see cleanly and the one the market pays for. It is not a judgement on the player's career, and a player who stayed and improved is not a miss in any football sense.
