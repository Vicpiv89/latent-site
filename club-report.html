Reading the excerpts

Every crop below comes from one published Club Report (Oleh Kozhushko measured against Dynamo Kyiv), rendered in the report's own type.

01

Level vs target team

Is he an upgrade on what that club already has, or depth priced as an upgrade?

His output mapped directly onto the target squad: his per-90 numbers next to the club's current baseline in his position, the depth and workload already there, and where he would land in that squad's internal ranking on the metrics that define his role.

When the two sides sit in different tiers, that is the first thing the section says, and it then keeps the two profiles separate, each ranked inside its own competition, rather than inventing an adjustment factor to blend them.

02

Top 5 competitors

Whose minutes is he actually taking?

The players at the target club he would genuinely compete with, compared head-to-head: output, usage, age and minutes share. This is the section a negotiation turns on, because it replaces "we already have players there" with names and numbers.

03

Comparison vs league

How far is the destination's standard from his current one, and on which metrics is the gap real?

Context for the jump: his rates against the positional baselines of the target club's competition, so both sides can see the size of the step rather than argue about it.

Presented as raw-rate context with its limits stated. We never print cross-league percentiles, and we don't invent a tier-adjustment factor to make a comparison look cleaner than it is.

04

Comparison vs players

Against the other candidates for the same slot, who is better on the same metric set?

Head-to-head against named alternatives — the other players on the club's shortlist, or the statistical comparables we surface if there isn't one. Side-by-side profiles on one metric set, so the choice between candidates is an argument about numbers instead of reels.

Who it's for

Both sides of the table

Agents & intermediaries

Walk into the conversation holding the club's own squad numbers. When your player outranks their incumbents on the metrics that matter, this is how you prove it before the fee discussion starts.

Club staff

Pressure-test a recruitment case before committing to it. Whether the answer is "sign him" or "the options you already have are better", it arrives with the evidence attached.

Stated limits, in writing

Every Club Report closes what it cannot support: metrics the competition does not record, seasons outside our coverage, comparisons that would need an adjustment we have not validated. A missing number is reported as missing, never silently shown as zero. In a negotiation that matters twice over, the report the other side cannot poke a hole in is the one that ends the argument. How every number is made is public: our full methodology.
